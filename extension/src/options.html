⚡ AI footprint

See the energy, carbon, water and money behind your AI use, and get small nudges toward lighter habits.

Your goal

25 Wh is about 80 typical chat replies. Set 0 to turn the goal off. You get one gentle reminder the first time you pass it each day.

Apps to measure

If a site doesn't say which model answered, pick the closest one so the estimate uses the right size. ChatGPT reports its model automatically.

Your AI plans (optional)

For example "ChatGPT Plus, 20, USD" or "Gemini, 1950, INR". Plan cost is spread over each day and shown in your own currency.

Carbon intensity (optional)

This is about the data centres' electricity, not yours. The default is 0.40, a typical grid average where big AI data centres run. Change it only if you have a better figure for your provider.

Sync to a dashboard (optional, off by default)

Send daily totals (counts only, never text) to your own or your team's AI footprint dashboard.

Your data

Everything is stored only in this browser. The extension never reads or keeps what you type or what the AI says. It only measures how long each message is.

How we estimate

AI companies don't publish per-message energy, so these are careful estimates, shown with a likely range.

  1. Tokens. About 4 characters of text are 1 token. Each reply is charged for the whole conversation so far, because chat apps re-read it every time.
  2. Energy per token depends on model size:
    SizeExamplesWh per 1,000 tokens read / written
    SmallGemini Flash, GPT mini, Claude Haiku0.01 / 0.10
    MediumGPT-5, GPT-4o, Claude Sonnet, Gemini Pro0.05 / 0.50
    LargeClaude Opus0.12 / 1.50
    Extra largeDeep research, Claude Fable0.25 / 3.00
    A typical medium-model reply comes to about 0.3 Wh. That is in line with the figures Google (0.24 Wh per Gemini prompt) and OpenAI (0.34 Wh per ChatGPT query) published in 2025.
  3. Thinking models write hidden reasoning before replying, so we count their output 4×.
  4. Images: about 2.9 Wh per generated image (Luccioni et al., 2023).
  5. Carbon = energy × grid intensity. Water = energy × 1.1 L/kWh of data-centre cooling.

Not included: training the models, building the hardware, or your own device. The real figure could be roughly 0.4× to 3× our central estimate.
